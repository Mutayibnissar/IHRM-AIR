Unit I: Landing in IHRM

Morgan's model of IHRM
IHRM is the interplay of three things: HR activities (procurement, allocation, utilisation), countries (host, parent, other) and employee types (HCN, PCN, TCN). Example: IBM employs Britons in Britain (HCNs) and sends Americans to Asia (PCNs).
PCN, HCN, TCN and expatriates
PCN = citizen of HQ country; HCN = host-country citizen; TCN = neither. An expatriate works temporarily abroad; an inpatriate is subsidiary staff moved to HQ.
Domestic vs international: 6 factors
  • More HR activities (tax, relocation, permits)
  • Broader perspective (equity across nationalities)
  • More involvement in personal lives and family
  • Changing PCN/HCN mix as subsidiaries mature
  • Risk exposure: failure can cost about 3x salary
  • Broader external influences (law, economy, custom)
Same activities, higher complexity.
4 moderating variables
  • Culture: culture shock, emic vs etic, convergence vs divergence
  • Industry: multidomestic vs global (Porter); Laurent's 5 steps
  • Home-market reliance: big home market means less global thinking
  • Senior management attitude: build a global mindset
Strategic IHRM framework
De Cieri and Dowling: external factors (PESTLE, asymmetric events like terrorism) and organisational factors (integration vs local responsiveness, structure, strategy, culture) shape the HR function and MNE performance.

Unit II: Staffing and Performance

EPRG staffing approaches
  • Ethnocentric: PCNs run key posts; strong control, blocks HCN careers
  • Polycentric: HCNs run units; cheap and stable, risk of a 'federation'
  • Geocentric: best person, any nationality; costly and slow
  • Regiocentric: moves within a region only
PCN vs TCN vs HCN
PCNs give control but cost more; TCNs can be cheaper and regionally savvy but face rivalries; HCNs avoid language and permit issues but loosen HQ control.
International assignments
Reasons: position filling, management development, organisation development. Types: short-term, extended, long-term; non-standard: commuter, rotational, contractual, virtual, self-initiated.
Expatriate failure and selection
  • Failure = premature return (broader: underperformance too)
  • Top causes: spouse or manager cannot adjust, poor selection
  • Selection: technical ability, cross-cultural suitability, family, country rules, MNE needs, language
  • U-curve: honeymoon, culture shock, adjustment, mastery
Do not pick on technical skill alone.
Performance and appraisal
Five variables: compensation, task, HQ support, host environment, cultural adjustment. PCN roles cross one cultural boundary, TCN roles two. Appraise with hard, soft and contextual criteria. For HCNs, adapt to culture (Japan: save face) and involve locals.

Unit III: Paying People Abroad

Objectives: firm vs employee
The firm wants strategy fit, attraction and retention, cost-effective transfers and equity. The employee wants protection (benefits, living costs), financial gain, and housing, schooling and home leave covered. Core tension: global standardisation vs local customisation.
The expatriate package
  • Base salary (the foundation)
  • Foreign service premium and hardship: usually 5-40% of base
  • Allowances: cost-of-living, housing, home leave, education, relocation, spouse assistance
  • Benefits and leave (home leave, R&R)
Going rate approach
Pay follows the host country market. Plus: equal to locals, simple. Minus: pay varies by posting, re-entry problems.
Balance sheet approach
Most used. Aim: keep the expatriate whole on home pay plus inducement. Four outlays: goods and services, housing, income taxes, reserve (savings). Fair and easy to explain, but creates disparities and is complex to run.
Local Plus, tax and pay variables
  • Local Plus: host pay plus perks; cheaper, but retention risk
  • Tax equalisation is the most common policy; also tax protection
  • Pay is shaped by internal factors (strategy, size, culture) and external ones (law, unions, labour market, culture)
Exam favourite: going rate vs balance sheet.

Unit IV: Unions, Law and Trends

Comparative industrial relations
Concepts differ by country (collective bargaining in the USA is local; in Germany and Sweden it is industry-wide). Union types: industrial, craft, conglomerate, general, enterprise. Density is high in Sweden, Denmark, Norway and low in France and the USA; OECD average fell from 18.8% to 16.9% (2005-2013).
MNE industrial relations policy
IR is usually delegated, but HQ steps in more with high production integration, US ownership, young or strategic subsidiaries, poor performance and anti-union attitudes. The type of MNE matters, not multinationality itself.
How unions limit MNEs
  • Four limits: wages, employment levels, global integration, campaigns
  • Kennedy's concerns: huge finances, alternative supply, mobility, remote HQ, many industries, IR expertise, investment strike
Union responses and global bodies
International trade secretariats (limited success), lobbying for laws, regulation through the ILO and OECD Guidelines (chapeau clause, Badger case), and corporate campaigns (James Hardie). Transnational bargaining stays remote.
Regional integration: the EU
Rome 1957, Social Charter 1989, Single Market 1992, 2004 enlargement, Lisbon 2009. Worry: social dumping, a race to the bottom on labour costs.
Emerging trends
  • Codes of conduct (SA 8000; Rana Plaza showed weak enforcement)
  • Offshoring: India (high turnover), China (guanxi), nearshoring
  • Ethics: relativism vs absolutism vs universalism; bribery laws (FCPA, OECD)
  • Safety, security and counter-terrorism now sit with HR

Self-test — think, then reveal

  • Name the 6 factors that make IHRM more complex.

    More activities, broader perspective, personal-life involvement, changing PCN/HCN mix, risk exposure, external influences.

  • Compare ethnocentric and polycentric staffing.

    Ethno: PCNs, HQ control, but blocks HCNs. Poly: HCNs run units, cheap and stable, but the firm becomes a federation.

  • List 6 expatriate selection factors.

    Technical ability, cross-cultural suitability, family, country rules, MNE needs, language.

  • Going rate vs balance sheet?

    Going rate uses host pay and equals locals; balance sheet uses home pay to keep the expatriate whole.

  • 4 ways unions limit MNEs?

    Wage levels, employment levels, global integration, campaigns.

  • Why is offshoring an HR issue?

    Turnover, skill shortages, culture and language, control, plus consulting unions and redeploying staff.
